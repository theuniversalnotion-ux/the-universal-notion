✦ Case Study

Behind the Build

How I designed, priced, and launched a modular Notion template store — from positioning to the first sale.

By Ella 10 products · $4.99 each · bundle $14.99

01 — Goal

What I set out to do

I wanted to build a real digital product from scratch — something that combined a genuine problem I cared about (my own chaotic Notion workspace) with skills I was building: design, positioning, and a bit of product thinking.

The goal wasn't just to make templates. It was to create a store that felt designed — with a strong point of view, a scrapbook aesthetic, and pricing that was easy to say yes to.

02 — Audience

Who this is for

The primary audience is Notion users who want their workspace to feel as good as it looks — people who spend time on aesthetics, who love a well-organized system, and who don't want to build from scratch every time.

Secondary audience: people discovering Notion for the first time who want a ready-made structure they can duplicate and use immediately, without setup friction.

03 — Brand & Positioning

The aesthetic as the strategy

Most Notion template stores feel like productivity tools — clean, minimal, functional. I positioned The Universal Notion in the opposite direction: warm, handcrafted, scrapbook-coded. The palette (rose and espresso), the tilted cards, the washi tape, the pushpins — everything signals that this is made with care, not mass-produced.

The "block" framing was deliberate: instead of selling templates, I'm selling modular pieces of a workspace. It implies you can pick what you need, mix and match, and build something uniquely yours — which is true, and it's a more compelling story.

The name "The Universal Notion" nods to both the Notion app and the idea that there's a universal system for living — an organized, beautiful one.

04 — Pricing Strategy

$4.99 each. $14.99 for all.

The pricing structure is intentional. Each block is priced at $4.99 — low enough to be an impulse buy, high enough to signal that it's a real product, not a freebie. The $4.99 price point is the psychological sweet spot for digital micro-products: no real deliberation required.

The bundle is $14.99 for all 10. Buying individually would cost $49.90 — a 70% discount. The bundle serves two functions: a better deal for committed buyers, and an anchor that makes individual blocks feel cheap by comparison.

Option Price Logic
1 block $4.99 Impulse-buy threshold. Low commitment, easy yes.
All 10 blocks (bundle) $14.99 70% off — strong anchor vs individual pricing. Appeals to buyers who want everything.

05 — Launch & Channels

How I got the word out

The store was built as a static site on GitHub Pages with a custom domain (theuniversalnotion.com). All purchases go through Gumroad, which handles payments and delivery.
